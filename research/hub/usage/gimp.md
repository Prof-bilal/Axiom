# GIMP

## When to use
- You need free, open-source software for photo retouching, image composition, or image authoring.
- You need pixel-level work such as cloning, healing, dodging and burning, or painting on bitmaps.
- You need layer-based compositing with masks, layer groups, and blend modes.
- You need to export images for the web in formats such as PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, or HEIF.

## How it works
- The Toolbox holds the tools and their options; the Image Window shows the picture being edited.
- Layers, Channels, Paths, Brushes, and Undo History are dialogs that dock or float beside the image.
- Selections (rectangle, ellipse, lasso, fuzzy select, foreground select, scissors) limit where edits apply; they can be feathered or edited in Quick Mask.
- Filters are grouped by effect (Blur, Distort, Noise, Artistic, and others) and apply to the selected layer.

## Not for
- Vector illustration and logo design: GIMP works on pixels; use a vector editor such as Inkscape (general guidance, not stated on the pages used).
- Page layout or print production: Not found in the sources used.

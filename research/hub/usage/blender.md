# Blender

## When to use
- You need 3D modeling, sculpting or painting on meshes.
- You are animating characters or objects, including rigging and physics.
- You need rendering or compositing of 3D scenes.
- You are making 3D assets for games or motion graphics and want one tool for the whole pipeline.

## How it works
- The interface is built from workspaces, which are arrangements of editors called areas.
- Main editors include the 3D Viewport, Outliner, Properties, Timeline, Dope Sheet, Graph Editor and Shader Editor.
- The 3D Viewport works in modes (such as Object, Edit and Sculpt) that decide what your actions change.
- Scenes contain objects; editors show and change those objects in different ways.
- The built-in Help System gives tooltips and opens the manual for the current context.

## Not for
- Simple 2D drawing or painting; use Krita or Inkscape.
- Quick video cutting without 3D work; use Kdenlive.
